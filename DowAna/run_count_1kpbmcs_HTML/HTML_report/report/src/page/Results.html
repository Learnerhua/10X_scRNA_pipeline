1 Technology Introduction

This project uses the single-cell omics technology (Chromium)[1], which was developed by 10X Genomics to analyze gene expression patterns at the single cell level from target samples.

The experimental processes are as follows:

1. Sample processing and quality inspection: Target tissue samples were prepared with cell/nuclear suspension and evaluated for cell/nuclear suspension quality control.

2. Isolation of single cells and capture of mRNA molecules: The qualified cell/nuclear suspension was processed by the droplet microfluidic device of Chromium to generate GEMs (Gel beads in Emulsion) and realize the separation of single cells. mRNA molecules were recovered by demulsification after  GEM 
GEM
Gel Beads-in-Emulsion, the core technology of 10X Chromium platform. Through microfluidic chips, single cells and gel beads carrying oligonucleotide tags are encapsulated in water-in-oil droplets, forming independent micro-reactors.
 generation, and cDNA was synthesized by reverse transcription of mRNA molecules.

3. Library preparation and sequencing: The cDNA library was constructed and sequenced after synthesis.

Data analysis:

quality control is performed for sequenced data, and a full set of bioinformatic analyses are conducted to obtain the gene information expressed at the cell/nucleus level of the target sample.

5 High variable gene selection and PCA dimensionality reduction

5.1 Highly variable feature screening

Based on the mean and dispersion (mean and variance) of all genes in each sample, R-package -Seurat[3] was used to screen out genes with high variability in each sample for downstream PCA analysis. The 2,000 genes with the highest degree of variation are selected by default. See Information Analysis Flow - High Variable Feature Screening for details.
Figure 4  High variant gene screening plot.   
The X-axis represents the average expression level, and the Y-axis represents the standardized variance. On the basis of the left figure, marker genes with the top 10 expression level variation were marked in the right figure.

5.2 Principl component analysis

Dimensional heat maps show the heterogeneity of principal component (PC) genes with the greatest variation and their expression levels in cell data, helping to select appropriate dimensions for downstream analysis. You can also use the elbow plot to determine how many PCS are used to cluster in order to capture most of the changes in the data. Figure 5 and Figure 6 are used to show the dimension reduction results of top15PC. It can be seen that with the increase of PC components, features become less and less obvious, which is helpful for downstream selection of appropriate dimensions.
Figure 5  Dimension reduction heatmap for Top15 PCS.   
Each column represents a cell and each row represents a gene; Yellow indicates high expression and purple indicates low expression.
Figure 6  Elbow plot for Top15 PCS.   
The X-axis is the PC serial number and the Y-axis is the standard deviation.

6 Data Clean

Different libraries of a single sample were cleaned and filtered separately, and then combined for subsequent analysis after meeting the quality control standards. By calculating the possible proportion of double cells (nuclear) in the droplet and the possible foreign RNA contamination, the cells most likely to be contaminated or double cells (nuclear) were inferred by hypothesis testing under a certain probability. The results of the calculation are presented by the dimensionality reduction cluster plot of the data, and the cells eliminated by both filtering methods are eliminated and not included in the subsequent analysis.
Figure 7  Doublets (nuclear) and Singlets cells identified by DoubletFinder.   
Each dot represents a cell. Different colors indicate cell types; red indicates that the cell is Doublets (nuclear) and cyan indicates that the cell is Singlets.
Figure 8  DecontX identifies contaminated cells.   
Each dot represents a cell. Different colors indicate cell types, with red indicating a higher probability of cell contamination and cyan indicating a lower probability of cell contamination.

According to the quantitative results, the data were filtered out, and the cells that might be double (nuclear) or with contamination were filtered out. The filtered data set was used to homogenize the expression levels of all genes in the cells for subsequent analysis.

9 Annotation of Cell Clusters

After dividing cells into different groups through cluster analysis, cell types are further identified by using marker gene information in public databases.

SingleR Cell Annotation

SingleR[3]can recognize the existing cell clustering results based on Seurat, compare each cluster with a reference data set, and score the cluster according to the similarity of gene expression between cluster and known cell groups in the reference data set to obtain a scoring matrix. Cells/Bins can be annotated into different groups by scoring matrix results. 10 Pseudotime Analysis

Pseudotime analysis, also known as trajectory inference, refers to obtaining cell lineage information according to the differentially expressed genes among different cell groups and constructing a change trajectory of cells in a virtual timeline. Based on these results, a cell change timeline can be recreated.

Monocle3[4] was used for Pseudo-time analysis. Based on the result of dimensionality reduction and clustering, we further estimated whether the clustering results belonged to the same developmental trajectory. The cell groups were visualized according to the developmental trajectories predicted by the software, and the results are shown in Figure 17.
Figure 17  Cell development trajectory diagram.   
The black lines in the figure show the presumed trajectory of cell development.
According to the results of cell development trajectory, combined with the changes of gene expression levels in different cell groups, Monocle3 could identify the cells at the earliest developmental stage using a machine learning algorithm and create a pseudo-time analysis, as shown in Figure 18.
Figure 18  Diagram of pseudo time series analysis results.   
The black lines represent the presumed developmental trajectory of the cells, ranging in color from purple to yellow, and the color closer to yellow, the cells develop the later.

In the clustering results of different cell classes, the top 5 marker genes calculated by Monocle3 were selected, and the gene expression levels were visualized according to the obtained pseudo-time results, as shown in Figure 19.
Figure 19  Pseudo expression result of Marker gene.   
x-axis, the result of Pseudotime; y-axis, gene expression, and the color ranges from purple to yellow. the color closer to yellow, the cell development time is the later. The black line shows trends in gene expression.

11 Interaction of Cell Clusters

CellChat[5] is a tool capable of quantitatively inferring and analyzing cell-to-cell communication networks from single-cell data, using network analysis and pattern recognition methods to predict the main signal inputs and outputs of cells and how these cells and signals coordinate their functions. The CellChatDB database incorporates signal analysis information from KEGG and recent studies. The database includes known ligand-receptor complex compositions, including ligand-receptor polymeric complexes and several coenzyme factors. In this project, CellChat was used to identify ligand/receptor relationships and intercellular communication molecules in spatio-temporal data, and to study the interaction and communication networks between different cell types.The communication network among all cell clusters is shown in Fig20, and the communication network between each cluster and other clusters is shown in Fig21.
